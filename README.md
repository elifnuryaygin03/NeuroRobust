# NeuroRobust

### Multimodal Brain MRI Tumor Segmentation and Small-Lesion Robustness Analysis

**NeuroRobust** is a deep learning research and portfolio project for **binary whole-tumor segmentation** in multimodal brain MRI. The project uses the BraTS2021 dataset, a 2D U-Net, patient-level data splits, and systematic validation and independent held-out testing. In addition to evaluating segmentation quality, it studies small-tumor loss during mask downsampling and the trade-off between false-positive reduction and small-lesion detection.

> **Research/educational use only.** This is not a diagnostic tool, has not undergone clinical or external-dataset validation, and should not be used for medical decisions.

## Highlights

- **Four MRI modalities:** T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR.
- **Model:** 2D U-Net with approximately **7.77 million parameters**; input size **128 × 128 × 4**, output **128 × 128 × 1**.
- **Patient-level split:** 875 training, 188 validation, and 188 held-out test patients, with no patient-ID overlap.
- **Complete evaluation:** 29,140 slices from 188 validation patients and 29,140 slices from 188 held-out test patients.
- **Error analysis:** small-lesion detection, empty-slice false positives, metric aggregation, resizing artifacts, and post-processing.

## Dataset and preprocessing

**Dataset:** BraTS2021 brain MRI scans (access subject to the dataset provider's terms). The raw MRI scans and annotations are **not redistributed** in this repository.

1. Load the four MRI sequences for each subject in the order **T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR**.
2. Apply a per-volume, nonzero-voxel Z-score normalization to each modality.
3. Resize axial MRI slices to **128 × 128** with bilinear interpolation (`INTER_LINEAR`).
4. Convert nonzero tumor segmentation labels into a **binary whole-tumor mask**; resize masks with nearest-neighbor interpolation (`INTER_NEAREST`).
5. Evaluate patients using separate training, validation, and held-out test patient-ID sets.

The project uses **2D slice-wise predictions**, not a native-resolution 3D network. A 3D *patient-volume Dice* score is calculated by pooling the resized 2D masks across a patient's slices; it should not be confused with a full-resolution 3D model result or BraTS subregion metrics.

## Model and evaluation

- **Architecture:** U-Net-style encoder–decoder with skip connections.
- **Inference:** Pixel-wise sigmoid probability map, binarized at a **0.5** threshold.
- **Primary metric:** Mean binary Dice across tumor-containing slices.
- **Additional metrics:** Binary IoU, small-tumor Dice (1–10 tumor pixels in the resized mask), background false-positive slice rate, and mean patient-volume Dice.
- **Post-processing comparison:** No filtering versus removal of isolated connected components smaller than **2 pixels** (chosen using validation data, then held fixed on the test set).

**Metric interpretation:** Tumor-slice Dice, mean all-slice Dice, pooled Dice, and mean patient-volume Dice use different aggregation rules. Results are labeled accordingly; they should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Final quantitative results

### Full validation set — 188 patients, 29,140 slices

| Metric | Baseline U-Net | 2-pixel component filter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean Dice on tumor-containing slices | **0.8414** | 0.8411 |
| Mean IoU on tumor-containing slices | 0.7665 | 0.7663 |
| Mean Dice on small-tumor slices (1–10 pixels) | 0.2572 | 0.2519 |
| Background false-positive slice rate | 27.44% | **24.58%** |

### Independent held-out test set — 188 patients, 29,140 slices

| Metric | Baseline U-Net | 2-pixel component filter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean Dice on tumor-containing slices | **0.8434** | 0.8432 |
| Mean IoU on tumor-containing slices | 0.7711 | 0.7709 |
| Mean Dice on small-tumor slices (1–10 pixels) | 0.2526 | 0.2480 |
| Small-tumor slices with no detected tumor pixels | 43.33% | 44.68% |
| Background false-positive slice rate | 29.33% | **26.45%** |
| Mean patient-volume Dice (resized binary masks) | 0.8891 | 0.8894 |

**Interpretation:** Held-out test tumor-slice Dice (0.8434) was close to validation Dice (0.8414), suggesting fairly consistent performance within this dataset split. The 2-pixel filter reduced background false positives by **2.88 percentage points** on the held-out test set, while slightly reducing small-tumor sensitivity. These results do **not** establish generalization to independent hospitals or external datasets.

## Investigating disappearing small tumors

A discrepancy was identified between tumor-containing slices indexed at the original resolution and masks after resizing:

- Original validation slices marked as tumor-containing: **12,445**.
- Tumor-containing masks after resizing to 128 × 128: **12,390**.
- **55 slices** contained a tumor at the original resolution but had an empty mask after nearest-neighbor downsampling.

A separate preprocessing experiment compared nearest-neighbor resizing with area-based alternatives:

| Approach | Previously lost tumor-containing slices recovered | Effect on additional control masks |
|---|---:|---|
| Nearest neighbor | 0 / 55 | Baseline |
| Area-based threshold > 0.10 | 55 / 55 | Increased foreground mask areas in the evaluated controls |
| Selective one-pixel recovery | **55 / 55** | **30 / 30 sampled control masks unchanged** |

**Selective recovery** is a **label-preprocessing proof of concept**: when nearest-neighbor downsampling produces an empty mask despite a nonempty original mask, the method assigns one pixel to the output location with the greatest tumor coverage. Its benefits and geometric accuracy need broader verification. **It was not incorporated into the U-Net checkpoint used for the final reported test results**, and it should not be interpreted as an improvement in model segmentation performance.

## Connected-component filtering: the trade-off

Filtering small isolated predicted regions lowers false-positive rates, but can also erase genuine small-tumor predictions. On the complete validation set, a 2-pixel minimum component threshold offered a conservative trade-off: background false-positive slices dropped from **27.44% to 24.58%**, while mean tumor-slice Dice changed from **0.8414 to 0.8411**. More aggressive thresholds produced larger losses in small-tumor sensitivity.

The **0.5 prediction threshold** and **2-pixel minimum component size** were fixed before evaluating the held-out test set.

## Limitations and future work

- **Small tumors:** Performance on 1–10-pixel lesions remains limited, and some tumor-containing masks become empty after downsampling.
- **Preprocessing trade-offs:** Area-preserving and selective rescue methods must be assessed for geometric fidelity, not only for whether a mask is nonempty.
- **Binary segmentation only:** The model does not separately predict BraTS tumor subregions (e.g., enhancing tumor, tumor core).
- **Internal evaluation:** A patient-disjoint held-out split was used within BraTS2021; **external-site generalization and clinical usefulness have not been established**.
- **Further experiments:** Evaluate higher-resolution or 3D methods, compare against stronger architectures, report uncertainty or confidence intervals, and assess robustness across patient subgroups.
